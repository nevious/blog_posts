A 8-day trip to Scotland shortly after the [Edinburgh Festival Fringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_Festival_Fringe) in August. We experienced it as quite touristy, perhaps on account of [Outlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlander_(TV_series)) as well as the arriving shortly after the Fringe. We've enjoyed our time and especially the people and will be visiting again!

We came back thinking _"If people were more like the Scots, the world would probably be a better place"_. It can be as easy as _"Hey! How are you guys?"_.

Credit to the entire trip goes to the missus, who planned, booked and prepped the entire thing. All _I_ had to do was sit in a plane.

## Edinburgh

One of the friendliest and most inclusive cities I've visited. We were introduced to Scotland by an old lady bumping her trolley into us followed by "Oh dear I apologize". This sort of genuine friendliness kept resonating throughout our trip and has made us deeply appreciative of the Scottish people.

### The Royal Mile

Reaching from **Edinburgh Castle** to the **Palace of Holyroodhouse** is the **Royal Mile**, spanning multiple streets. Don't be looking for street signs, look for Pub signs instead. It's an interesting stroll as a lot of Edinburgh's history has taken place here. However we came to recognize it as a tourist hotspot. It is also a perfect spot to see the Glaswegian-born but now Scot-identifying tradition of putting traffic cones on statues. I think it originally started via football and is now something that has caught on, perhaps for a variety of reasons. Much to my amusement and approval.

### Mary Kings Close

The city under the city. I won't be spoiling the tour as it's quite entertaining. I highly recommend spending the few pounds to visit this site!

### Port of Leith Distillery

A young whisky distillery, because it was someones dream. As was explained; the founders were met with a few challenges, amongst which the need for land, its price and the solution to "go vertical" in their building process. Also worth a recommendation to spend a few pounds here and visit the place, taste some spirits and maybe take a bottle home with you. The Port presented to us was my personal favorite.

### Museums

There are a lot of museums and a fair amount of tourists enjoy them. While being big, some being big enough to require multiple visits. We went to the National Museum of Scotland as well as the Surgeons' Hall Museum. While the Surgeons' museum is visually quite taxing (mind your stomach!), it is very interesting. The National Museum of Scotland was honestly too big to go in blind!

### Pubs

On retrospective, I guess the best way to recommend a pub is not to check the reviews, but simply checking how old the establishment is. Scots love to have a drink and socialize. If the place has been open for a while, you're good to go!

### Platform 41

This place comes with a special recommendation. A place with a 5-star review on google surely has to be a fake. However if I ever saw a place earning such a rating, it's this one. Not because it's good service - it is, not because the sandwiches and pastries are excellent - they are, not because the coffee is amazing - it is, not because the staff is friendly but because all metrics of what makes a good place a good place have been turned up to 11 and perhaps even beyond.

It's an incredibly small place, barely seating more than 10 people. The staff is incredibly attentive and appears to build a relationship with every customer coming in. Simply starting with "Hey guys, how are you?" and - from what I can tell - genuinely expecting you to tell them how you are, continuing with a lighthearted chat. Small talk in a very big way. Ordering almond milk the first day and oat milk the next, is something that did not go unnoticed and was double checked. We felt very welcome every time we went there. We went there a lot.

## Inverness

For us, Inverness was a means to an end. The end being the Highlands and the Culloden Battlegrounds.

### Culloden

There really is something special about standing on a field. Only once you've learned that a lot of people died on it of course! It's the more-or-less final piece of the [Jacobite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobitism) portion of Scottish history. If you're into that, it's a day well spent. The museum on-site is really nicely structured and breaks the Revolution into digestible pieces while still providing both sides of the conflict.

## Highlands

I'll let the pictures speak for themselves. While throwing myself into driving on **the wrong side of the road** was certainly a challenge, I'd do it again in a heartbeat! It's as pretty and as wide as the movies make you believe. However careful evaluation of what to visit is advised and in any case navigating the onslaught of tourist is going to be a factor.

Speaking on the issue of driving; I've only gotten honked at once! Sorry to the guy I cut off!

### Stirling

Stirling castle is also worth the visit. It's another place for history-nerds centering around [Mary of Guise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_of_Guise) and [James V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_V). Other than that, it's a castle - there's no other framing than this. You certainly can get the history lesson of Wikipedia, however standing in the castle that was the center of Scottish rule is an experience in itself.
